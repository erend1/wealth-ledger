# WealthLedger Data Capture Guide

Status: Proposed canonical capture requirements

Last reviewed: 2026-08-24

## Purpose

This guide defines the source facts that user workflows and future importers
should capture. It is a product-level checklist, not a database schema. Domain
rules, accepted ADRs, and implemented use cases remain authoritative for exact
storage and arithmetic.

The central rule is:

> Record evidence-backed facts once; derive balances, values, gains, and
> allocation from them.

## Data classification

Every candidate field belongs to one of four classes:

| Class | Meaning | Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| Source fact | Appears on a statement, receipt, contract, physical item, or explicit user decision | Persist with provenance where useful |
| Classification | Adds stable economic meaning, such as contribution category or cost treatment | Persist as explicit stable vocabulary |
| Derived result | Can be reproduced from source facts, such as position or fine-gold weight | Calculate; do not make authoritative mutable state |
| Analysis or proposal | Assumption, forecast, target, or agent suggestion | Store separately from posted ledger facts |

Unknown evidence is not zero. A blank value must not be converted into a
fabricated amount, date, price, or acquisition cost.

## Common transaction facts

Capture these when applicable to every economic event:

- household;
- transaction type;
- order, execution, and settlement business dates;
- portfolio purpose;
- source and destination accounts;
- institution or counterparty;
- asset and quantity delta;
- cash consideration and currency;
- executed unit price and price currency;
- external source reference;
- human note;
- evidence reference or import provenance;
- household-member attribution when relevant;
- typed cost components and their treatments;
- creation and posting timestamps generated by the system.

The system generates internal identities and retry/idempotency metadata. Users
may inspect them diagnostically but should not need to invent them.

## Contribution

Required source facts:

- destination cash account and portfolio;
- currency asset;
- positive amount;
- contribution date;
- cash-flow category.

Optional facts:

- contributing household member;
- originating institution or description;
- external reference;
- note and evidence reference.

A contribution is external capital entering the ledger. It is not the purchase
of a fund or gold asset.

## Withdrawal

Capture the source cash account and portfolio, currency asset, positive entered
amount displayed as an outgoing effect, business date, category, reference,
note, and evidence. It is external capital leaving the ledger and is not a sale.

## Fund purchase

Capture:

- fund identity and lot-tracking policy;
- destination investment account and portfolio;
- source cash account;
- acquired unit quantity;
- execution date;
- total cash consideration;
- executed unit price and price currency;
- order and settlement dates when known;
- commission, tax, brokerage, or other costs with treatment;
- institution, external reference, note, and evidence.

Preserve both executed price and cash consideration as source facts. If their
mathematical relationship differs because of rounding, fees, settlement, or bad
input, show and resolve the discrepancy explicitly rather than overwriting one
with the other.

## Fund sale

Capture:

- fund and source account/portfolio;
- disposed unit quantity;
- execution and settlement dates;
- gross or net cash proceeds, with an explicit semantic contract;
- executed unit price and price currency;
- fees and withholding with treatment;
- lot-selection policy or explicit allocations when the accepted workflow
  requires them;
- institution, external reference, note, and evidence.

Realized cost is derived from lot allocations. It is not a user-entered sale
field.

## Physical-gold purchase

Capture:

- physical-gold asset or product identity;
- physical-vault account and portfolio;
- seller or institution;
- purchase date;
- gross weight;
- actual fineness;
- piece count;
- total cash consideration and currency;
- source cash account when tracked;
- making charge and whether it is included in consideration or an additional
  cash outflow;
- hallmark, certificate reference, product/form label, note, and evidence when
  available;
- other fees or taxes with treatment.

Derive fine-gold weight from gross weight and fineness. Do not duplicate it as
an independently editable authoritative field.

Jewelry wording such as "no making-charge loss" is not a numerical source fact.
Actual executable buy and sell observations are market/reference data and must
be captured separately if spread analysis is desired.

## Physical-gold sale

Capture:

- exact lots or deterministic allocation policy;
- disposed gross quantity and pieces where whole-piece rules apply;
- buyer or institution;
- sale date;
- cash proceeds and currency;
- observed execution price;
- fees, taxes, assay deductions, or other deductions with treatment;
- reference, note, and evidence.

The workflow must prevent disposal beyond the effective remaining lot quantity.
Any whole-piece restriction must be a tested product rule, not an assumption
derived from PieceCount alone.

## Transfer

Capture source and destination accounts, source and destination portfolio
purposes when different, asset, quantity, transfer date, lot allocations for
lot-tracked assets, reference, note, and evidence.

An internal transfer preserves total household quantity and acquisition
lineage. It does not create income, return, or a new acquisition price.

## Opening balance

Capture:

- as-of date;
- account, portfolio, asset, and quantity;
- lot breakdown where required;
- known historical acquisition date and cost only when evidenced;
- explicit Unknown cost when evidence is absent;
- physical-gold details for each distinct lot;
- statement, inventory count, or other provenance.

Opening market value, when entered for inception analysis, remains a dated
valuation observation and must not masquerade as historical cost.

## Adjustment

Capture the exceptional reason, signed quantity effect, business date, account,
portfolio, asset, approving user, reference, note, and evidence. Adjustment is
not a convenience path for ordinary purchase, sale, transfer, or correction.

## Cost components

For each cost, capture:

- type;
- non-negative amount and currency;
- treatment;
- note or evidence when useful.

Treatments must answer whether the cost is an additional cash outflow, withheld
from proceeds, already included in consideration, or informational only. This
prevents double counting.

Spread is not a cost component. It is derived from comparable bid and ask
observations.

## Market and reference observations

Capture:

- instrument or asset;
- observation timestamp and effective business date;
- bid, ask, close, net asset value, exchange rate, or other explicit quote type;
- value, unit, and quote currency;
- source/provider;
- whether the value was manual, imported, or provider-supplied;
- freshness and quality metadata;
- correction or supersession relationship when a source revises data.

Do not overwrite a historical quote merely because a newer quote exists.

## Goal and policy facts

Capture separately from the ledger:

- goal name and purpose;
- target amount and currency;
- target date, date range, or explicitly flexible horizon;
- minimum liquid reserve;
- permitted asset universe;
- target allocation ranges and concentration limits;
- loss tolerance and risk-reduction schedule;
- review frequency;
- effective date and policy version;
- rationale and approval.

Changing a policy does not rewrite the policy that governed a historical
decision.

## Decision journal

A decision entry may capture:

- decision date and review date;
- decision owner;
- question being answered;
- alternatives considered;
- facts and valuation date used;
- assumptions;
- selected action or reason for no action;
- expected portfolio role;
- risk limits or invalidation conditions;
- links to later actual transactions;
- retrospective outcome and lesson.

An agent suggestion is labeled as generated analysis and remains unposted until
a human submits a validated command.

## Reconciliation evidence

Capture:

- statement or physical-count effective date;
- source institution or custody location;
- observed quantities and cash;
- comparison run timestamp;
- differences and tolerance;
- evidence reference;
- resolution status;
- linked corrective transaction or documented explanation.

Never resolve a difference by editing a posted transaction in place.

## Derived values that users must not enter as truth

Do not create routine editable fields for:

- current balance or position;
- remaining lot quantity;
- average purchase price;
- current portfolio value;
- current profit or loss;
- current allocation percentage;
- fine-gold weight;
- realized cost basis;
- performance return.

These are query results with an effective date, method, and provenance.

## Units, precision, and boundary conversion

Human interfaces accept decimal-formatted values and convert them deliberately
to Domain representations. They must validate scale, overflow, sign, currency
precision, and rounding before posting. Binary floating point is never used for
authoritative financial conversion.

Importers must preserve the original source text or evidence reference when a
rounding or normalization decision could later matter.

## Minimum provenance rule

Every manually entered or imported transaction should be explainable later by
at least one of:

- institution and external reference;
- receipt or statement reference;
- physical inventory evidence;
- explicit opening-balance note;
- explicit adjustment rationale.

The application should support references without copying sensitive source
documents into source control or agent context.
